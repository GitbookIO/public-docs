# What "published" means: spaces, sites, and access

When you finish writing, editing, or importing content, you publish it for a selected audience. A docs site's content lives in its [sections](../publishing/site-sections-and-variants/README.md) — the structure you edit is the structure visitors get. New sections start as drafts, so you control exactly what appears on your published site.

Publishing a site doesn't change who can *edit* it — editing permissions are separate from an audience's read access, and are managed independently in a site's **Members** settings. Only your primary content branch publishes, too: any [change requests](../collaboration/change-requests/README.md) in progress stay private until merged.

### Audience options

A published site can be:

* **Public** — anyone on the web can view it, and it can be indexed by search engines (optional). See [Publish and unpublish a site](publish-and-unpublish.md#public-publishing).
* **Private, with share links** — anyone with a private link can view it in read-only mode, without needing to join your organization. See [Publish and unpublish a site](publish-and-unpublish.md#private-publishing-with-share-links).
* **Behind authenticated access** — visitors must sign in through your identity provider. See [Authenticated access](../access/authenticated-access/README.md).

These options aren't mutually exclusive with [adaptive content](../access/adaptive-content-concepts.md) — a public or share-linked site can still tailor what it shows based on visitor data.
